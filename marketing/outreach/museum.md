# Washington Township Museum of Local History

**info@museumoflocalhistory.org · (510) 623-7907**
190 Anza Street, Fremont (Niles) — check hours before turning up.

Why them, in order of what they are worth to you:

1. They hold the Tri-City photograph archive — Centerville, Irvington,
   Niles, Alvarado, Decoto, Warm Springs, Mission San José, from 1797 to
   the 1980s.
2. They already publish a **Then and Now** series, which is the same idea
   as one of your five games. That is either a content partnership or an
   awkward collision, and it is much better to be the person who wrote
   first.
3. They are the people who can tell you which images are actually out of
   copyright and which are not — that is a research question you would
   otherwise be guessing at.

Their site states **no** reuse or licensing policy, so this has to be
asked rather than assumed. Do not use anything of theirs until somebody
there says yes in writing.

## What to have ready when they reply

- **Which images.** Vague requests get vague answers. Go through the
  collection and list the specific photographs you want.
- **What the site is.** One sentence: a free daily puzzle about Fremont,
  no advertising, nothing sold, made by a local teacher. Being plainly
  non-commercial is the strongest thing you have to say to an archive —
  it is the difference between a request they weigh and a request they
  wave through.
- **Credit.** Offer it before they ask. A visible credit and a link is
  cheap for you and is the main thing a small archive wants.
- **Get it in writing.** An email saying yes, naming the images and the
  use, is the record. Save it.

## Ask on the call, not by email

Whether the images they hold from before 1931 are ones they consider
public domain. Publication date, not the date the photograph was taken,
is what decides it — and for a lot of local material nobody has ever
worked out whether it was published at all. They will know more about
their own collection's provenance than any general rule will tell you.
