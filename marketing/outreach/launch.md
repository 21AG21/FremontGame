# Launch channels

Ordered by what a hyperlocal daily puzzle actually needs, which is not
reach — it is the right thousand people. A puzzle about Fremont is
worthless to somebody in Ohio and close to essential to somebody who
drives past Mission Peak every morning.

## The one advantage you have

You are a Fremont teacher who built a Fremont thing. That is the story,
and it is the reason local press will run it and local groups will not
treat you as spam. Lead with it every time. Do not describe yourself as a
startup.

## 1. Nextdoor — do this first

Highest concentration of the exact audience, and it costs nothing.

- Post from your own account, in your own neighbourhood, then share to
  nearby ones.
- Use `wide-1200x630.png` if it renders the link card, `square-1080.png`
  if you are posting the image directly.
- Do not write an advertisement. Write: "I'm a teacher here and I made a
  little daily puzzle about Fremont. Today's asks you to name a place
  from a drawing. Curious how many people get it."
- Reply to every comment for the first week. This is the whole game on
  Nextdoor.

## 2. The local press

Real outlets, in the order most likely to answer:

- **Tri-City Voice** — Fremont/Newark/Union City weekly, covers exactly
  this, small enough to reply.
- **Fremont Patch** — patch.com/california/fremont, runs community
  submissions.
- **The Argus / East Bay Times** — bigger, slower, worth one email.
- **Fremont Chamber of Commerce** newsletter — they carry community
  items and reach exactly the people who have lived here longest.

Draft below.

## 3. Where Fremont already talks to itself

- **r/Fremont** on Reddit. Read the rules first; some subs ban
  self-promotion outright and posting anyway will get you banned rather
  than downvoted.
- Facebook: the Fremont community and "grew up in Fremont" nostalgia
  groups. The nostalgia groups are the best fit you will find anywhere —
  Then & Now is built for exactly that audience.
- The Fremont Main Library and the Niles Essanay Silent Film Museum both
  have engaged local followings and no reason to compete with you.

## 4. Schools

You work in one. Teachers, the staff room, the district newsletter, the
PTA lists. Still worth asking whoever owns a channel before posting to
it, but this got much simpler when the site stopped selling anything — a
free puzzle about local history with no advertising on it is the kind of
thing a district newsletter exists to carry.

## Draft — local press

> **Subject:** Fremont teacher built a daily puzzle about Fremont
>
> Hello,
>
> I teach in Fremont, and I've spent the last few months building a free
> daily puzzle site about the town — five short puzzles every morning,
> all of them specifically about Fremont. One asks you to name a place

> sort into groups. One shows you a scene and asks what year it is.

> collect anything — the puzzles are worked out on your own phone.
>
> Everything on it is drawn rather than photographed, and the places are
> real: Mission San José, the Centerville depot, Niles Boulevard, the
> peak. I built it because my students and my neighbours know this town
> and there was nothing that treated it as worth knowing.
>
> Happy to talk, send screenshots, or explain how it's put together if
> that's interesting. I'm local and easy to reach.
>
> [Your name]
> [Phone]

Attach `square-1080.png` and `wide-1200x630.png`. Do not attach four
images; two is a pitch and four is a burden.

## What not to do

- Do not buy ads yet. You have no idea what your retention is. Paid
  traffic on a leaky bucket buys you a number and teaches you nothing.
- Do not post the same text to six places on the same day. Nextdoor
  first, watch what happens for a week, then widen.
- Do not launch anywhere until the site's own metrics can tell you
  whether people came back the next day. That is the only number that
  matters for a daily puzzle, and it is the one thing you cannot
  reconstruct afterwards.
